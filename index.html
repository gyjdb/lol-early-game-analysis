01 · Question

How much does an early lead matter?

The central question is simple: how strongly is a Blue-side gold advantage at 15 minutes associated with eventually winning? To avoid double-counting, each observation represents one professional match, viewed from the Blue team's team-summary row.

Gold advantage

golddiffat15 measures Blue gold minus Red gold at minute 15.

Resource context

XP and CS differentials describe whether the lead is broad or concentrated.

Outcome

result is a binary target: Blue-side win or loss.

Key finding

Blue-side wins averaged a +1,874.78 gold advantage at minute 15, while losses averaged −1,533.13 gold.

Blue-side wins are shifted toward more positive 15-minute gold differentials, but the overlap shows an early lead does not determine the outcome.
Gold and experience advantages move together, but not perfectly—evidence that multiple early-game signals can add predictive value.
02 · Missingness

Missing data has a game-mechanics explanation.

A 25-minute timeline value cannot exist if the match already ended. That makes game length a natural observed explanation for missing goldat25, rather than evidence of an inherently MNAR mechanism.

Game length matters

Permutation p-value: 0.0002. Missingness depends strongly on observed match duration.

Side does not

TVD permutation p-value: 1.0000. Timeline availability is shared by Blue and Red summaries of the same match.

Matches missing a 25-minute gold value are shorter on average, which matches the timeline-generation process.
03 · Inference

The early gold signal is statistically strong.

I used a one-sided permutation test with the difference in mean 15-minute gold differential between Blue wins and Blue losses as the statistic. The observed gap was 3,407.92 gold.

Interpretation

The result supports a strong association between larger early gold advantages and eventual victory. It does not establish causation.

04 · Prediction

From descriptive signal to predictive model.

The prediction task uses only information available by minute 15 to classify whether the Blue-side team will win. Final-game and post-15-minute statistics are excluded to prevent leakage.

01
Split

Fixed stratified 80/20 train-test split.

02
Baseline

Median imputation + scaling + logistic regression.

03
Engineer

Kill diff, plate diff, and relative gold lead.

04
Tune

5-fold GridSearchCV across linear and nonlinear classifiers.

The final model improves on the baseline while preserving a simple, interpretable decision function.

I compared predictive accuracy in playoff matches against regular-season matches using a one-sided permutation test. The question was whether playoff accuracy is meaningfully lower.

The observed playoff-minus-regular-season accuracy difference is +1.02 percentage points, not evidence of worse playoff performance.
06 · Takeaways

What this project shows.

A compact summary of the analytical story, from raw match logs to a calibrated predictive model.

Early gold is informative—not deterministic.

The outcome distributions overlap substantially, so comebacks and conversion efficiency still matter.

Simple models remain competitive.

After comparing nonlinear alternatives, regularized logistic regression still produced the best cross-validated result.

Feature engineering adds incremental value.

Combat, structural pressure, and relative economic scale improved held-out accuracy above the baseline.

Evaluation should match deployment context.

The fairness check explicitly tests whether model quality changes in higher-stakes playoff games.
